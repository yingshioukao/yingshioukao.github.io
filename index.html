About Me

I am an Adjunct Assistant Professor in the Department of Political Science at Soochow University, Taiwan. My research focuses on parliamentary studies and democratic accountability, examining how parliaments interact with political elites, citizens, and civil society to shape democratic governance.

My work spans three interconnected areas: parliamentary institutions and legislative oversight, political behavior and representation, and the role of civil society in parliamentary accountability. Drawing on quantitative, qualitative, and experimental methods, I explore these dynamics through comparative research, primarily focusing on Japan and Taiwan.

I hold Ph.D. degrees in Political Science from Soochow University and Chinese Studies from Aichi University, Japan. I previously held postdoctoral research positions at Soochow University and Academia Sinica. Beyond academia, I have conducted policy research for the Westminster Foundation for Democracy and contributed to Taiwan's open parliament initiatives aligned with the principles of the Open Government Partnership (OGP).
